Jodhpur, the Blue City

A fort on a cliff, a maze of indigo-washed houses below it, and the Thar Desert beyond. Here is what to see, eat and plan.

Plan your days

The gateway to the Thar

Rao Jodha founded Jodhpur in 1459 as the capital of the Marwar kingdom. He built his fort on a rocky ridge that rises sharply from the plain, and the city grew in the shadow of its walls.

Many of the old-city houses are painted a soft indigo. Locals say the colour helps keep homes cool and keeps insects away, and it was once linked with Brahmin households. Today it is simply what makes the view from the fort so recognisable.

Jodhpur is the second-largest city in Rajasthan, and a good base for desert villages, lakes and temples.

Places worth your time

Start with the fort, then mix palaces, gardens and the old bazaar.

Mehrangarh Fort

One of the largest forts in India, standing on a cliff high above the city. Inside are palace courtyards, carved jharokha windows, a museum of howdahs, palanquins and weapons, and ramparts lined with old cannons. Take the audio guide and walk the walls for the best views of the blue houses.

Allow 3 hours. Go at opening time to avoid crowds and heat.

Jaswant Thada

A white marble memorial built in 1899 for Maharaja Jaswant Singh II. The thin marble panels glow when light passes through them, and the lawns around it are quiet compared with the fort.

Short visit, about 45 minutes. Often combined with the fort.

Umaid Bhawan Palace

A sandstone and marble palace completed in 1943, now part royal residence, part luxury hotel and part museum. The museum section shows royal photographs, clocks and a collection of vintage cars.

Museum open to visitors. Check timings before you go.

Mandore Gardens

Mandore was the capital of Marwar before Jodhpur. Today its terraced gardens hold red sandstone cenotaphs of past rulers, a shaded place to walk in the afternoon.

About 9 km north of the city centre.

Clock Tower and Sardar Market

The Ghanta Ghar is the centre of old-town life. Around it, Sardar Market sells spices, textiles, bandhani scarves, silver and Rajasthani juttis. Bargain politely and compare prices between stalls.

Best in the evening. Many shops close on Sundays.

Toorji Ka Jhalra

An 18th-century stepwell near the clock tower, restored and surrounded by cafés. Its symmetrical stone steps make a good photo stop.

Light is best in the morning.

What to eat

Jodhpur food is rich, spicy and often fried. Pair it with something sweet or cool.

Mirchi bada

A large green chilli stuffed with spiced potato, dipped in gram flour batter and fried. Eat it hot, usually with chutney.

Pyaaz and mawa kachori

Flaky fried pastry with an onion filling, or a sweet version stuffed with milk solids and soaked in syrup.

Makhaniya lassi

A thick, saffron-flavoured lassi topped with cream. Famous near the clock tower and very filling.

Laal maas and ker sangri

A fiery mutton curry and a desert bean and berry dish. Ask for a milder version if you are not used to chilli.

Best time

October to March has mild days. Summers regularly pass 40°C, so plan sightseeing for early morning.

Getting around

Autos and cabs work well in the city. The old town is best explored on foot.

Dress and etiquette

Wear comfortable shoes and light, covering clothes. Remove footwear at temples.
